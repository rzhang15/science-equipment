\section*{Appendix: Empirical Bayes shrinkage of market-level price effects}

Each of the 46 matched treated markets $m$ yields a difference-in-differences
estimate $\hat\beta_m$ of the post-2014 change in log prices, with standard
error $\widehat{se}_m$ (university $\times$ market $\times$ year panel,
2013-spend weights, errors clustered by market). Because the coefficients are
estimated market by market---some from thin cells---the raw cross-market
distribution overstates the true dispersion in price effects, and the noisiest
estimates would otherwise enter the exposure measure at face value. We
therefore apply normal--normal empirical Bayes shrinkage. The sampling model
is $\hat\beta_m \mid \beta_m \sim N(\beta_m, \widehat{se}_m^{\,2})$ and the
prior is $\beta_m \sim N(\mu, \tau^2)$, with the prior parameters estimated
from the cross-market distribution: $\hat\tau^2$ by method of moments,
$\hat\tau^2 = \max\{0,\ \mathrm{Var}(\hat\beta_m) -
\overline{\widehat{se}_m^{\,2}}\} = 0.031$, and $\hat\mu$ as the
precision-weighted ($1/\widehat{se}_m^{\,2}$) mean of the $\hat\beta_m$,
$\hat\mu = 0.18$ (precision weighting places more mass on tightly estimated
markets, which have larger price increases than the unweighted mean of
$0.07$). The posterior mean shrinks each estimate toward $\hat\mu$ in
proportion to its noise,
\[
\hat\beta^{EB}_m = w_m \hat\beta_m + (1 - w_m)\,\hat\mu,
\qquad
w_m = \frac{\hat\tau^2}{\hat\tau^2 + \widehat{se}_m^{\,2}},
\]
with posterior standard error $\widehat{se}^{EB}_m = \sqrt{w_m}\,
\widehat{se}_m$. Estimated cross-market heterogeneity is large relative to
sampling noise---$\hat\tau^2 / (\hat\tau^2 +
\overline{\widehat{se}_m^{\,2}}) = 0.94$---so shrinkage is mild for most
markets: the median weight is $0.99$, eight of 46 markets have $w_m < 0.9$,
and the smallest weight is $0.60$. The average estimate moves by $0.011$ log
points, the largest move is $0.10$ (site-directed mutagenesis systems,
$-0.40$ to $-0.29$), the cross-market standard deviation falls from $0.182$
to $0.164$, and the correlation between raw and shrunk estimates is $0.996$.
The shrunk coefficients $\hat\beta^{EB}_m$ are the market-level shifts carried
into the shift-share exposure measure. The same procedure is applied to the
market-level spending coefficients ($\hat\tau^2 = 0.093$, $\hat\mu = 0.37$),
which are used only descriptively.
